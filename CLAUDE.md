# Mosaicus Maximus: Claude Code Guide

A cozy craft and business game in Godot 4.7 (GDScript, 2D, GL Compatibility). You run a Roman mosaic workshop: design floors for clients, lay them tile by tile, grout and polish them, and restore old floors found under the town. The design is in `Overview.md`.

## Where things are

- `game/scripts/autoload/`: `Dev` (command-line options, screenshots and the made-up workshops of `--demo-state` and `--max-state`), `Data` (everything in `game/data/*.json`), `Audio` (sounds by name with `Audio.play`; it also plays the player's own .ogg, .mp3 and .wav files from `user://music/` between the game's tracks, as Farmus Maximus does; Settings' Open folder button opens that folder), `Settings` (everything in `user://settings.cfg`; `Settings.changed` redraws an open settings window), `Game` (the workshop's state), `Saves` (three slots, saved every minute and on close: each save goes to a `.tmp` file that is read back and then moved over the last, which joins three rotating backups; `before_save()` on the current screen adds to the save first, as the floor screen does with the tiles laid so far), `Nav` (screens and the three layers; Escape and the pad's B close the newest open window marked by `Ui.closes_with` (each is numbered as it opens or shows again), or call the screen's `go_back()`; a click beside such a window closes it too, through a clear `Backdrop` under it that keeps the click, except for the guide's notes, the chapter letters and the title's New workshop page, which pass `outside` false so a stray click loses nothing; controls in `Backdrop.ABOVE`, such as the workshop's top bar, still take their own clicks), `Tips` (tooltips at the interface's scale; give a control one with `Ui.tip_on`, never `tooltip_text`; Settings can turn them off), `Awards` (checks `Achievements` every second, slides in a card for each one earned that stays five seconds or until clicked, holding it while any window is open (`Nav.window_open()`, which leaves out the guide's notes) and sliding it in once they close, and sends them to Steam through GodotSteam when Steam is running, but only for a real workshop: `Awards.may_send()`; every second it also works out the Steam rich presence, `Awards.presence()`, a `steam_display` token from `tools/steam/rich_presence.vdf` (all fourteen languages, uploaded by hand as `docs/Steam-setup.md` says) with `%client%` or `%day%`, and sends it only when it changes and only while Steam runs).
- `Pad` (the controller, for the Steam Deck): the left stick moves the pointer and A clicks and drags, B is Escape, X and Y turn and mirror a motif, LB and RB size the brush, the d-pad's up and down and the right stick send mouse-wheel clicks at the pointer (scrolling a list, zooming the floor, sizing a motif on the plan), the d-pad's right is Tab (next stone) and View is C (the chalk). `Pad.GLYPHS` names the button picture for each key, drawn by `make_ui.py` into `assets/ui/pad/` in the interface's ink and walnut, 13 pixels across with 5 by 7 letters and a cream rim so they read on the grey floor and dark wood; `PadGlyph.badge` pins one beside a hint (the floor's brush and chalk buttons, the stone hint, the design table's turn and mirror) and shows it only while `Pad.using`, and the Keys tab lists them, with a With a controller group, while a controller is plugged in. `--pad` shows the game as with a controller in hand, for screenshots (with `--no-mouse`). The whole game was checked at 1280 by 800 with it.
- `game/scripts/design/`: `Stones` (the 16 stones), `FloorDesign`, `Composer` (design to colour map), `MotifLibrary`, `BorderPainter`, `MosaicLayout` (colour map to tiles), `RandomDesign`, `Samples`.
- `game/scripts/floor/`: `FloorView` (every tile in two batches, plus grout, polish, dirt and repairs), `FloorScreen`, `FloorHud` (the first nine stone chips show their number key small in a corner, in Silkscreen, hidden while `Pad.using` says a gamepad was used last).
- `game/scripts/business/`: `Brief` (wishes, and `judge` for a job's whole verdict), `Looks` (the five qualities clients see, tastes and stars), `Pricing`, `JobFactory` (commissions, copies, contests, restorations), `Story` (finishing jobs, chapters and what each brings, days, ships, learned tastes), `Achievements` (from `data/achievements.json`, judged from the save's `stats` and floors), `Damage`, `Library` (kept floors in `user://library/`: picture, thumbnail, design and laying order).
- `game/scripts/screens/`: title, workshop, job board, client visit, design table, reveal, day end, town map, library.
- `game/scripts/ui/`: `UiTheme`, `Ui` helpers (`Ui.window_head` and `Ui.close_button` for a window's corner X, `Ui.setting` and `Ui.on_off` for a settings row, `Ui.tab_row` and `Ui.tab_button` for paper tabs), `PlanView`, `MotifIcons`, `LoadingCard`, `SettingsPanel` (tabs Sound, Display, Gameplay and Keys), `ChaptersPanel` (the chapters as an open book: a numbered bookmark per chapter, the story and the chapter's first picture on the left page, what it brings on the right, sized from `Nav.ui_size()`; a laurel bookmark after them lists the achievements over as many spreads as they need, and a sealed letter bookmark the letters and news read so far, newest first, from the save's `letters`), `PageTurn` (the turning leaf between spreads, the same file as Farmus Maximus's and Fishus Maximus's; a page's drawings must live in exported values, since the turn pictures a duplicate of the page), `Hotspot` (a clickable workshop piece that lights up), `DogView`, `DogChooser`, `DogPanel`, `Guide` (the first day's notes, one per screen, shown once each; `Guide.active()` ends them after the first floor or on Skip the guide; the corner `"over"` lays a note on a control, as narrow as fits its height, as the design table's lies on the brief to keep the plan clear), `EdgeFade` (fades each edge of a picture smaller than the window into the background where the window reaches past it, as the workshop's room does, so it never ends in a straight cut).
- `tools/art/`: `make_ui.py` (interface art), `make_motifs.py` (the pattern book from `motif_sources/`; the guard dog is drawn by hand in `draw_dog()` and pictures in `DARK` are lightened first, so no picture merges with its black outline), `pixellab.py` and `make_art.sh` (PixelLab art, costs logged in `ledger.csv`), `make_workshop.sh` (furniture inpainted into the empty room, one step per piece) and `make_workshop_layers.py` (cuts each piece out against the empty room into `assets/art/workshop/` with `layers.json`, and carries the room on past the painting, 52 room pixels at each side and 16 below, so a 21:9 window shows room rather than dark bars (the ceiling fades into the dark): PixelLab paints it without the room as context into `sources/workshop/wide.png`, which is kept, and `widen()` says how; `--redo-wide` paints it again; `layers.json`'s `room` is where that wider picture lies, measured from the painting's corner like the pieces). The PixelLab token is in `.env`, never in code. `unused/` holds art the game no longer uses.
- `tools/art/make_rooms.py`: the clients' rooms; `--preview` draws a room's shape and masks without painting. A wall mosaic's room puts the mosaic in an arch on the back wall, and an apse's corners and an atrium's pool are painted with the room. It draws each room's walls and floor in the room's own proportions, so the floor's corners are exact, then PixelLab paints the walls and each piece of furniture; the floor is never repainted, and where it still shows the painting is left clear. Corners go to `assets/art/rooms/rooms.json`.
- `game/data/people.json`: everyone in town who isn't a regular client: 20 townsfolk (each with a fixed name, trade and face), the collectors, the people who find old floors, the neighbour and the contest herald. Each has a `look` for their portrait; `tools/art/make_portraits.py` paints the missing ones (`--dry-run` lists them). Until then `Data.face` borrows an old townsfolk face of the same sex.
- `tools/art/make_icon.py`: the game's icon (`game/icon.png` for the window, `game/icon.ico` for the exe and taskbar), rounded into a tile with a dark rim as Fishus Maximus's is, from a small mosaic the game lays itself with `--path game -- --no-save --icon=RENDER.png` (`--icon-motif`, `--icon-stones`, `--icon-border`, `--icon-frame`, `--icon-ground`, `--icon-size`, `--icon-room` change it; `--icon-stones=own` keeps the motif's own stones, and `--icon-allowed=A,B,...` swaps them for the nearest of those, to see a picture in one chapter's stones). `marketing/profile_picture.png` is the same renderer's flower in a roundel, 1080 by 1080, rounded like the icon by `make_icon.py --profile tools/art/profile_square.png` from the square render kept there, framed to read in a circle: `--icon=OUT.png --icon-motif=flower --icon-stones=own --icon-tiles=34 --icon-ring=33 --icon-size=24 --icon-fine --icon-res=1080 --icon-border= --icon-ground=white_marble` (`--icon-tiles` sets the square's size, `--icon-ring` adds a roundel round the picture, `--icon-fine` lays the picture in half-size tiles and `--icon-res` sets the picture's size in pixels).
- `tools/art/make_achievement_icons.py`: the Steam achievement icons in `store/achievements/`. `docs/Steam-setup.md` has the Steamworks steps and the achievement table.
- `tools/audio/cut_sounds.py`: cuts the game's sounds from `tools/audio/downloads/`. Sources and licences are in `CREDITS.md`, and the shipped credits in `game/data/credits.json`.
- `game/tests/`: `run_tests.gd` runs every `test_*.gd` file (each extends `test_case.gd`), and a test that raises an engine error fails; `test_logic.gd` holds the game's tests, and `test_memory.gd` goes round the screens (all but the day's end, which moves the story on) and the workshop's windows five times and checks the later rounds leave no extra objects, nodes, orphan nodes or resources, and turns the chapters book's pages, some turns cut short by closing it, to check the same. `flow_bot.gd` plays one job through every screen, and `bench.gd` times the heaviest scenes.
- `game/translations/strings.csv`: every player-facing string, one column per language (English plus French, German, Spanish, Italian, Brazilian Portuguese, Polish, Turkish, Russian, Ukrainian, Japanese, Korean, and Simplified and Traditional Chinese). `python tools/extract_strings.py` rebuilds it, keeping existing translations: it collects `tr()`, `TranslationServer.translate()` and `Ui.say()` strings, every literal that reads like words, the shown fields of `data/*.json`, and the forms built from names (lower-case names, motifs with their article, client wishes). `python tools/translate_strings.py` fills empty cells with Azure AI Translator (key `AZURE_TRANSLATOR_KEY` in `.env`; `--dry-run` shows what is missing), then `--headless --path game --import` turns each column into a `.translation` file. Azure gets short interface words wrong without context (Settings as scenery, Quit as resigning, Ground as a playing field, floor as a storey, polish as the language), so every language was reviewed line by line against the code that shows it; new strings need the same review.

## Fixed decisions

- `Nav` draws three layers. Floors render at full resolution in `Nav.world`. Painted pictures made for a 480 by 270 view (the workshop's room, the town map, the clients' rooms) go in `Nav.pictures`, scaled by `Nav.picture_scale()`: the smallest whole number that covers the window if that crops at most 12% a side, else the largest that fits, with `EdgeFade` softening any edge. The interface is pixel art in `Nav.ui`, one scale step finer than what fits the window (`Nav.ui_scale_for`, as Farmus Maximus does it: three times at 1080p, so 640 by 360), except on windows 800 pixels tall or less (`Nav.SMALL_HEIGHT`: 720p, the Steam Deck's 1280 by 800, a 1366 by 768 laptop), which take what fits, twice at both, since a step finer is too small to read there; every screen and window was checked at 1280 by 720 and 1280 by 800 in German and Russian, a step larger or smaller with the Interface size setting (`Settings.ui_scale_step`: Smaller -1, Normal 0, Larger 1, Largest 2), never so large that 480 by 270 won't fit. Anything on the interface that points at a picture converts with `Nav.picture_to_ui`.
- A tile is 6 design pixels; picture panels use half-size tiles through the composer's fine mask.
- Every balance number lives in `game/data/balance.json`. Pacing is checked by `test_pacing_report`.
- Briefs only ask for motifs, tags and stones the player has unlocked (`JobFactory.possible`).
- The workshop is one painted room with every piece cut out of it, so pieces share its light and pixel size. New pieces are inpainted into it, never drawn as separate sprites. A clickable piece's outline and click area come from a shape traced by hand in `SHAPES` in `make_workshop_layers.py`, because the inpainting also changes the shadows, wall and floor round it.
- Two rooms in `rooms.json` are not ordinary floors: `panel` (`"bench": true`), the square a picture panel is laid on, which no client asks for, and `niche` (`"wall": true`), a wall mosaic in chapter 4, never dug up or copied. `Data.job_rooms()` is the list clients choose from. A panel set into a floor is a placement with a `panel` id: it keeps its size and stones, `Composer.panel_mask` marks it, it needs no stone, and the floor screen lays it at once (`FloorView.prelay`). Designs are shown with `Composer.picture`, which leaves the outside of an apse or pool clear; `Composer.compose` is the colour map the layout reads.
- Festivals (`data/festivals.json`) come every tenth day (`festival` in balance.json), and the dog finds a dig some mornings (`dog`); both are in `Story.morning`.
- The reveal shows the floor in the client's room: one painting per kind of room (`assets/art/rooms/`), a cutaway seen from above the front, with a clear gap where the floor is. `RoomView` draws the real floor behind that gap in perspective (`Perspective`, `room_floor.gdshader`) from a live `FloorView` in a SubViewport, and the client's card slides in on the left. No people in the room. A room without a painting yet falls back to the floor alone in its walls.
- A floor's score is 30% wishes and 70% looks (`Looks.judge`), and the weakest quality pulls the looks down. The design table judges with only the taste the player knows (`Story.known_taste`); the reveal uses the client's real taste.
- The player lays every tile, one stone at a time: the brush lays only the stone picked in the tray, nothing is picked for them, and its places glow (`FloorView.guide`). Every floor tool is picked by the player too: the trowel to dig, a stone (one tile a click) or the chisel to mend, then Done, and the sponge and cloth to grout and polish. Mended stones travel to the reveal and library (`apply_stones`).
- `FloorView.prepare(layout)` makes a floor's mortar, chalk and tile data on the worker thread that built the layout, so `setup` takes a few milliseconds instead of a quarter of a second; a screen that builds a layout calls it in the same task. A screen that starts a `WorkerThreadPool` task hands it to `Nav.leave_task` in `_exit_tree`, since a task nobody waits for is never freed and keeps its whole floor in memory.
- The floor draws nothing it doesn't need: the flying batch is hidden once every tile has landed, the stone guide while no stone is picked, the grout film and polish sheen until the floor is first wiped, and the dirt once dug, and a settled tile's shader stops at once outside the 0.64 tile widths its stone and shadow cover. Any change to the floor's look is checked against renders of the old code made with the same fixed clock.
- Behind other windows the game runs at 30 frames a second (`Settings.pace`), except while recording. Quitting goes through `Audio.quit`, which stops every sound first, since a sound still playing at exit is never freed.
- `Nav.go` drops every listener the last screen connected to `Nav.resized` and `Game.changed`; inline functions left connected crashed the game on the next window resize.
- The clock runs while a workshop is played (`time` in balance.json). The workshop multiplies its room by `Daylight.light`, draws `SkyView` through the cut-out sky in `room.png`, and lights every workshop picture in its shader (`workshop_light.gdshaderinc`: the hour's daylight plus warm pools round the lamps). Lamps burn only after dark (`Daylight.lamps_lit`): their flames and painted glow are a layer of their own, `lamps_flame.png`, split off by `make_workshop_layers.py`. `room_sun.png` is the light map: red is painted sunlight, which fades at night, and green the outdoor view, which lamps don't light. A workshop picture's shader must take its tint from `COLOR` in `vertex()` and call `workshop_light`: in `fragment()`, Godot 4's incoming `COLOR` already includes the texture, so using it there squares the colours and hides anything drawn on clear pixels.
- Every letter and piece of news is kept in the save's `letters` once read (`Story.keep_letter`, with its day), as the `Ui.say` words it was shown in, for the chapters book's Letters page. A save from before letters were kept rebuilds only its chapter letters (`Story.chapter_letters`), with no days.
- The free demo is the same project exported with the `demo` feature (presets `Windows demo`, `macOS demo`, `Linux demo`; `python tools/export.py --demo` builds them into `builds/<platform>-demo/`, without the launcher). `Game.is_demo()` reads it (`Game.acts_as_demo` for the tests, `--as-demo` to try it from the editor), and `Game.last_chapter()` is `demo.chapters` in balance.json, 2: the demo ends where chapter 3 would open (a keen player reaches it around day 10), since chapters 3 and 4 are exactly what its end card teases, a chapter's letter is a natural place to stop, and a day limit would cut a player off mid-chapter. `Story.check_chapters` then queues one `demo_end` event (`state.demo_over`), which the workshop shows as `DemoEnd`: thanks, four pictures from chapters 3 and 4, what comes next, Carry on, and Wishlist on Steam, which opens `steam://store/<id>` in a running Steam client or the store page otherwise, with the id from `demo.store_app_id` (hidden while 0; `publish.py demo` refuses to upload until it matches the game's App ID). The title shows Demo and the same button. `Story.unlock`, restorations' hidden patterns and the ships stop at the demo's last chapter, free design offers only its stones, patterns, borders and rooms, and anything past it reads "In the full game": the chapters book, locked patterns and borders, the top bar's tip (once the demo is over the bar shows the reputation alone, without the target of a chapter it never opens), `Story.next_chapter_line`, and a saved workshop past chapter 2 on the title. The save folder is the same, so a demo workshop carries on in the full game, whose workshop opens chapter 3 as soon as it loads (the workshop runs `check_chapters` on entering). Achievements are earned into the save as usual and reach Steam when the full game opens that workshop, as Farmus Maximus's demo does. `test_demo.gd` covers it.
- Chapters are always visible: the top bar's Chapter button opens `ChaptersPanel`, chapter letters list `Story.chapter_unlocks`, and the reveal and day end show `Story.next_chapter_line`. A new achievement needs only an entry in `achievements.json` (one condition key, read by `Achievements.met`), a picture in the icon script's `ART`, and a row in `docs/Steam-setup.md`.
- Every restoration hides one unowned pattern from up to a chapter ahead, which finishing it adds to the book.
- The dogs are the Farmus Maximus dogs (`assets/art/dogs/`), shown at their own size.
- Money is shown with `Ui.coins_text(n)` ("1 denarius", "12 denarii"), never "d". The game is set in AD 72 on the Bay of Naples.
- `Ui.button` centres a centred button's text on the whole button, keeping as much room on the right as its icon takes on the left (`Ui.centre_text`); left-aligned buttons keep their icon as it is.
- Every shown string goes through `tr()`, or `TranslationServer.translate()` in a static function; labels and buttons also translate a whole literal by themselves. Words kept in the save to be read later (job notices and client names, letters, kept floors) are stored as `Ui.say(format, parts)` and shown with `Ui.text()`, so they read in whatever language is chosen when they are shown; never store translated text. A name inside a sentence uses its own lower-case row (`Data.motif_name`, `Data.room_name`, `Data.border_name`, `Stones.lower`), a client's wish is one whole row (`Brief.wish_key`), he and she sentences are two rows, and lists are joined with `Ui.list`, since other languages change the words round them. `game/data/names.json`'s notices are whole sentences with `{name}` and `{room}`.
- Settings has a Language list of every translated column, each named in its own language (`SettingsPanel.NATIVE_NAMES`), kept as `language` in `settings.cfg`. Until one is chosen the computer's language is used if the game has it, else English. Choosing one rebuilds the screen (`Nav.reopen`).
- Fonts (`UiTheme`): text is Fusion Pixel 10 px, which has Polish, Turkish and Cyrillic letters; Chinese, Japanese and Korean use Fusion Pixel's 8 px fonts at 8 px; headings are Silkscreen in English, French, German, Spanish, Italian and Portuguese and the text font otherwise, and the game's name is always Silkscreen. Sizes come from `UiTheme.text_size()` and `heading_size()`, never fixed numbers. A label holding a translated sentence gets a wrap width (`Ui.label(parent, text, variation, width)`), and `Tips` breaks lines between letters in languages without spaces.
- A picture starts at 60% of the floor's short side on the design table (patterns at 34%), since a picture fewer than about 20 tiles across can't show its face or legs.
- A first-chapter picture's default stones may come from chapter 2; the design table swaps each for the nearest unlocked stone (`_stones_for`), so in black and white the dog's red collar and mouth turn black.

Every preset leaves out `tests/`, `tools/` and `steam_appid.txt`. The exe's and the Mac app's version come from `config/version` (1.0.0), with the studio, Slow Villa Games, as the company and copyright holder, and the Mac bundle id is `com.slowvilla.mosaicusmaximus` (`com.slowvilla.mosaicusmaximus.demo` for the demo); the macOS export needs `import_etc2_astc` on, since it is universal. The Windows preset uses a trimmed engine, `tools/engine/slim.windows.template_release.x86_64.exe` (about 32 MB instead of 107 MB, kept in Git LFS), built in Farmus Maximus by its `tools/engine/build_template.py` and copied from its `tools/engine/bin/`; `tools/engine/slim_profile.py` records what it leaves out. It has no 3D, physics, regex, noise, TLS or ZIP module, so the game makes its own noise (`SoftNoise`), and it loads GodotSteam like the full engine. The builds hold no tests, so check a new build with dev options, such as `--screen=floor --sample=villa --fill-at=2 --shot-at=8 --shot=OUT.png --quit`, which sets out a floor on a worker thread. The Linux build can be run in WSL Ubuntu with `GALLIUM_DRIVER=llvmpipe`, since WSL's own Direct3D 12 OpenGL driver crashes on the title screen and garbles colours.
- Release builds: pushing a `v1.0.0` tag, or Run workflow on the Actions tab, has GitHub test, export every platform of the game and the demo, sign and notarize the Mac apps, and for a tag publish a GitHub release (`.github/workflows/release.yml`). `docs/Release-builds.md` covers starting a build, where the builds appear, the Apple secrets and switching on the Steam upload.
- Steam: GodotSteam 4.24 (GDExtension, Steamworks SDK 1.65) is in `game/addons/godotsteam/`, cut to 64-bit Windows, macOS and Linux (its libraries are in Git LFS); each export puts its libraries beside the game, or in the Mac app's `Contents/Frameworks`. `Awards` starts it with `steamInitEx` and keeps it only when the status is 0, so the game plays as normal without the Steam client. A git-ignored `steam_appid.txt` holding 480 (Spacewar) beside the exe, or in `game/` for the editor, tests it before the game has its own App ID. `python tools/steam/publish.py playtest|game|demo [--dry-run]` builds every platform with a depot in `tools/steam/steam.json` (all 0 until Evan fills them in; a dry run then builds all three) and hands over to WSL (`wsl_build.sh`, in `~/mosaicus-steam`), which lays the builds out with their permissions, signs and notarizes the Mac app with rcodesign when Apple credentials are there (`apple_setup.sh`, entitlements in `macos_entitlements.plist`) and uploads with steamcmd (`wsl_setup.sh` installs both). `docs/Steam-publishing.md` has the setup.
