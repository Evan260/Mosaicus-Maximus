# Mosaicus Maximus: Claude Code Guide

A cozy craft and business game in Godot 4.7 (GDScript, 2D, GL Compatibility). You run a Roman mosaic workshop: design floors for clients, lay them tile by tile, grout and polish them, and restore old floors found under the town. The design is in `Overview.md`.

## Where things are

- `game/scripts/autoload/`: `Dev` (command-line options, screenshots and the made-up workshops of `--demo-state` and `--max-state`), `Data` (everything in `game/data/*.json`), `Audio` (sounds by name with `Audio.play`; it also plays the player's own .ogg, .mp3 and .wav files from `user://music/` between the game's tracks, as Farmus Maximus does; Settings' Open folder button opens that folder), `Settings` (everything in `user://settings.cfg`; `Settings.changed` redraws an open settings window), `Game` (the workshop's state), `Saves` (three slots, saved every minute and on close: each save goes to a `.tmp` file that is read back and then moved over the last, which joins three rotating backups; `before_save()` on the current screen adds to the save first, as the floor screen does with the tiles laid so far), `Nav` (screens and the three layers; Escape and the pad's B close the topmost window marked by `Ui.closes_with`, or call the screen's `go_back()`), `Tips` (tooltips at the interface's scale; give a control one with `Ui.tip_on`, never `tooltip_text`; Settings can turn them off), `Awards` (checks `Achievements` every second, slides in a card for each one earned that stays five seconds or until clicked, and sends them to Steam through GodotSteam when it is there, but only for a real workshop: `Awards.may_send()`).
- `game/scripts/design/`: `Stones` (the 16 stones), `FloorDesign`, `Composer` (design to colour map), `MotifLibrary`, `BorderPainter`, `MosaicLayout` (colour map to tiles), `RandomDesign`, `Samples`.
- `game/scripts/floor/`: `FloorView` (every tile in two batches, plus grout, polish, dirt and repairs), `FloorScreen`, `FloorHud`.
- `game/scripts/business/`: `Brief` (wishes, and `judge` for a job's whole verdict), `Looks` (the five qualities clients see, tastes and stars), `Pricing`, `JobFactory` (commissions, copies, contests, restorations), `Story` (finishing jobs, chapters and what each brings, days, ships, learned tastes), `Achievements` (from `data/achievements.json`, judged from the save's `stats` and floors), `Damage`, `Library` (kept floors in `user://library/`: picture, thumbnail, design and laying order).
- `game/scripts/screens/`: title, workshop, job board, client visit, design table, reveal, day end, town map, library.
- `game/scripts/ui/`: `UiTheme`, `Ui` helpers (`Ui.window_head` and `Ui.close_button` for a window's corner X, `Ui.setting` and `Ui.on_off` for a settings row, `Ui.tab_row` and `Ui.tab_button` for paper tabs), `PlanView`, `MotifIcons`, `LoadingCard`, `SettingsPanel` (tabs Sound, Display, Gameplay and Keys), `ChaptersPanel` (the chapters as an open book: a numbered bookmark per chapter, the story and the chapter's first picture on the left page, what it brings on the right, sized from `Nav.ui_size()`; a laurel bookmark after them lists the achievements over as many spreads as they need), `PageTurn` (the turning leaf between spreads, the same file as Farmus Maximus's and Fishus Maximus's; a page's drawings must live in exported values, since the turn pictures a duplicate of the page), `Hotspot` (a clickable workshop piece that lights up), `DogView`, `DogChooser`, `DogPanel`, `Guide` (the first day's notes, one per screen, shown once each; `Guide.active()` ends them after the first floor or on Skip the guide), `EdgeFade` (fades each edge of a picture smaller than the window into the background where the window reaches past it, as the workshop's room does, so it never ends in a straight cut).
- `tools/art/`: `make_ui.py` (interface art), `make_motifs.py` (the pattern book from `motif_sources/`; the guard dog is drawn by hand in `draw_dog()` and pictures in `DARK` are lightened first, so no picture merges with its black outline), `pixellab.py` and `make_art.sh` (PixelLab art, costs logged in `ledger.csv`), `make_workshop.sh` (furniture inpainted into the empty room, one step per piece) and `make_workshop_layers.py` (cuts each piece out against the empty room into `assets/art/workshop/` with `layers.json`, and carries the room on past the painting, 52 room pixels at each side and 16 below, so a 21:9 window shows room rather than dark bars (the ceiling fades into the dark): PixelLab paints it without the room as context into `sources/workshop/wide.png`, which is kept, and `widen()` says how; `--redo-wide` paints it again; `layers.json`'s `room` is where that wider picture lies, measured from the painting's corner like the pieces). The PixelLab token is in `.env`, never in code. `unused/` holds art the game no longer uses.
- `tools/art/make_rooms.py`: the clients' rooms; `--preview` draws a room's shape and masks without painting. A wall mosaic's room puts the mosaic in an arch on the back wall, and an apse's corners and an atrium's pool are painted with the room. It draws each room's walls and floor in the room's own proportions, so the floor's corners are exact, then PixelLab paints the walls and each piece of furniture; the floor is never repainted, and where it still shows the painting is left clear. Corners go to `assets/art/rooms/rooms.json`.
- `game/data/people.json`: everyone in town who isn't a regular client: 20 townsfolk (each with a fixed name, trade and face), the collectors, the people who find old floors, the neighbour and the contest herald. Each has a `look` for their portrait; `tools/art/make_portraits.py` paints the missing ones (`--dry-run` lists them). Until then `Data.face` borrows an old townsfolk face of the same sex.
- `tools/art/make_icon.py`: the game's icon (`game/icon.png` for the window, `game/icon.ico` for the exe and taskbar), rounded into a tile with a dark rim as Fishus Maximus's is, from a small mosaic the game lays itself with `--path game -- --no-save --icon=RENDER.png` (`--icon-motif`, `--icon-stones`, `--icon-border`, `--icon-frame`, `--icon-ground`, `--icon-size`, `--icon-room` change it; `--icon-stones=own` keeps the motif's own stones, and `--icon-allowed=A,B,...` swaps them for the nearest of those, to see a picture in one chapter's stones). `marketing/profile_picture.png` is the same renderer's flower in a roundel, 1080 by 1080, rounded like the icon by `make_icon.py --profile tools/art/profile_square.png` from the square render kept there, framed to read in a circle: `--icon=OUT.png --icon-motif=flower --icon-stones=own --icon-tiles=34 --icon-ring=33 --icon-size=24 --icon-fine --icon-res=1080 --icon-border= --icon-ground=white_marble` (`--icon-tiles` sets the square's size, `--icon-ring` adds a roundel round the picture, `--icon-fine` lays the picture in half-size tiles and `--icon-res` sets the picture's size in pixels).
- `tools/art/make_achievement_icons.py`: the Steam achievement icons in `store/achievements/`. `docs/Steam-setup.md` has the Steamworks steps and the achievement table.
- `tools/audio/cut_sounds.py`: cuts the game's sounds from `tools/audio/downloads/`. Sources and licences are in `CREDITS.md`, and the shipped credits in `game/data/credits.json`.
- `game/tests/`: `run_tests.gd` runs every `test_*.gd` file (each extends `test_case.gd`), and a test that raises an engine error fails; `test_logic.gd` holds the game's tests. `flow_bot.gd` plays one job through every screen.
- `game/translations/strings.csv`: every player-facing string, one column per language (English plus French, German, Spanish, Italian, Brazilian Portuguese, Polish, Turkish, Russian, Ukrainian, Japanese, Korean, and Simplified and Traditional Chinese). `python tools/extract_strings.py` rebuilds it, keeping existing translations: it collects `tr()`, `TranslationServer.translate()` and `Ui.say()` strings, every literal that reads like words, the shown fields of `data/*.json`, and the forms built from names (lower-case names, motifs with their article, client wishes). `python tools/translate_strings.py` fills empty cells with Azure AI Translator (key `AZURE_TRANSLATOR_KEY` in `.env`; `--dry-run` shows what is missing), then `--headless --path game --import` turns each column into a `.translation` file. Azure gets short interface words wrong without context (Settings as scenery, Quit as resigning, Ground as a playing field, floor as a storey, polish as the language), so every language was reviewed line by line against the code that shows it; new strings need the same review.

## Fixed decisions

- `Nav` draws three layers. Floors render at full resolution in `Nav.world`. Painted pictures made for a 480 by 270 view (the workshop's room, the town map, the clients' rooms) go in `Nav.pictures`, scaled by `Nav.picture_scale()`: the smallest whole number that covers the window if that crops at most 12% a side, else the largest that fits, with `EdgeFade` softening any edge. The interface is pixel art in `Nav.ui`, one scale step finer than what fits the window (`Nav.ui_scale_for`, as Farmus Maximus does it: three times at 1080p, so 640 by 360), a step larger or smaller with the Interface size setting (`Settings.ui_scale_step`: Smaller -1, Normal 0, Larger 1, Largest 2), never so large that 480 by 270 won't fit. Anything on the interface that points at a picture converts with `Nav.picture_to_ui`.
- A tile is 6 design pixels; picture panels use half-size tiles through the composer's fine mask.
- Every balance number lives in `game/data/balance.json`. Pacing is checked by `test_pacing_report`.
- Briefs only ask for motifs, tags and stones the player has unlocked (`JobFactory.possible`).
- The workshop is one painted room with every piece cut out of it, so pieces share its light and pixel size. New pieces are inpainted into it, never drawn as separate sprites. A clickable piece's outline and click area come from a shape traced by hand in `SHAPES` in `make_workshop_layers.py`, because the inpainting also changes the shadows, wall and floor round it.
- Two rooms in `rooms.json` are not ordinary floors: `panel` (`"bench": true`), the square a picture panel is laid on, which no client asks for, and `niche` (`"wall": true`), a wall mosaic in chapter 4, never dug up or copied. `Data.job_rooms()` is the list clients choose from. A panel set into a floor is a placement with a `panel` id: it keeps its size and stones, `Composer.panel_mask` marks it, it needs no stone, and the floor screen lays it at once (`FloorView.prelay`). Designs are shown with `Composer.picture`, which leaves the outside of an apse or pool clear; `Composer.compose` is the colour map the layout reads.
- Festivals (`data/festivals.json`) come every tenth day (`festival` in balance.json), and the dog finds a dig some mornings (`dog`); both are in `Story.morning`.
- The reveal shows the floor in the client's room: one painting per kind of room (`assets/art/rooms/`), a cutaway seen from above the front, with a clear gap where the floor is. `RoomView` draws the real floor behind that gap in perspective (`Perspective`, `room_floor.gdshader`) from a live `FloorView` in a SubViewport, and the client's card slides in on the left. No people in the room. A room without a painting yet falls back to the floor alone in its walls.
- A floor's score is 30% wishes and 70% looks (`Looks.judge`), and the weakest quality pulls the looks down. The design table judges with only the taste the player knows (`Story.known_taste`); the reveal uses the client's real taste.
- The player lays every tile, one stone at a time: the brush lays only the stone picked in the tray, nothing is picked for them, and its places glow (`FloorView.guide`). Every floor tool is picked by the player too: the trowel to dig, a stone (one tile a click) or the chisel to mend, then Done, and the sponge and cloth to grout and polish. Mended stones travel to the reveal and library (`apply_stones`).
- `Nav.go` drops every listener the last screen connected to `Nav.resized` and `Game.changed`; inline functions left connected crashed the game on the next window resize.
- The clock runs while a workshop is played (`time` in balance.json). The workshop multiplies its room by `Daylight.light`, draws `SkyView` through the cut-out sky in `room.png`, and lights every workshop picture in its shader (`workshop_light.gdshaderinc`: the hour's daylight plus warm pools round the lamps). Lamps burn only after dark (`Daylight.lamps_lit`): their flames and painted glow are a layer of their own, `lamps_flame.png`, split off by `make_workshop_layers.py`. `room_sun.png` is the light map: red is painted sunlight, which fades at night, and green the outdoor view, which lamps don't light. A workshop picture's shader must take its tint from `COLOR` in `vertex()` and call `workshop_light`: in `fragment()`, Godot 4's incoming `COLOR` already includes the texture, so using it there squares the colours and hides anything drawn on clear pixels.
- Chapters are always visible: the top bar's Chapter button opens `ChaptersPanel`, chapter letters list `Story.chapter_unlocks`, and the reveal and day end show `Story.next_chapter_line`. A new achievement needs only an entry in `achievements.json` (one condition key, read by `Achievements.met`), a picture in the icon script's `ART`, and a row in `docs/Steam-setup.md`.
- Every restoration hides one unowned pattern from up to a chapter ahead, which finishing it adds to the book.
- The dogs are the Farmus Maximus dogs (`assets/art/dogs/`), shown at their own size.
- Money is shown with `Ui.coins_text(n)` ("1 denarius", "12 denarii"), never "d". The game is set in AD 72 on the Bay of Naples.
- `Ui.button` centres a centred button's text on the whole button, keeping as much room on the right as its icon takes on the left (`Ui.centre_text`); left-aligned buttons keep their icon as it is.
- Every shown string goes through `tr()`, or `TranslationServer.translate()` in a static function; labels and buttons also translate a whole literal by themselves. Words kept in the save to be read later (job notices and client names, letters, kept floors) are stored as `Ui.say(format, parts)` and shown with `Ui.text()`, so they read in whatever language is chosen when they are shown; never store translated text. A name inside a sentence uses its own lower-case row (`Data.motif_name`, `Data.room_name`, `Data.border_name`, `Stones.lower`), a client's wish is one whole row (`Brief.wish_key`), he and she sentences are two rows, and lists are joined with `Ui.list`, since other languages change the words round them. `game/data/names.json`'s notices are whole sentences with `{name}` and `{room}`.
- Settings has a Language list of every translated column, each named in its own language (`SettingsPanel.NATIVE_NAMES`), kept as `language` in `settings.cfg`. Until one is chosen the computer's language is used if the game has it, else English. Choosing one rebuilds the screen (`Nav.reopen`).
- Fonts (`UiTheme`): text is Fusion Pixel 10 px, which has Polish, Turkish and Cyrillic letters; Chinese, Japanese and Korean use Fusion Pixel's 8 px fonts at 8 px; headings are Silkscreen in English, French, German, Spanish, Italian and Portuguese and the text font otherwise, and the game's name is always Silkscreen. Sizes come from `UiTheme.text_size()` and `heading_size()`, never fixed numbers. A label holding a translated sentence gets a wrap width (`Ui.label(parent, text, variation, width)`), and `Tips` breaks lines between letters in languages without spaces.
- A picture starts at 60% of the floor's short side on the design table (patterns at 34%), since a picture fewer than about 20 tiles across can't show its face or legs.
- A first-chapter picture's default stones may come from chapter 2; the design table swaps each for the nearest unlocked stone (`_stones_for`), so in black and white the dog's red collar and mouth turn black.

## Running and checking

- Godot: `/c/Users/evand/AppData/Local/Microsoft/WinGet/Packages/GodotEngine.GodotEngine_Microsoft.Winget.Source_8wekyb3d8bbwe/Godot_v4.7.2-stable_win64_console.exe`
- After adding a class or any picture or sound: `--headless --path game --import`.
- Tests: `--headless --path game -- --run-tests` must print `ALL TESTS PASSED`; `--only=NAME` runs the test files whose names contain NAME.
- Whole-job check: `--path game -- --flow=FOLDER --no-save` plays one job through every screen and saves screenshots.
- Any screen in another language: add `--locale=de` (or any column of the strings sheet); it is not saved. `test_fonts_have_every_letter`, `test_every_string_in_the_code_is_in_the_sheet` and `test_translations_keep_their_placeholders` check the translations.
- Any screen alone: `--path game -- --screen=NAME --no-save --shot-at=3 --shot=OUT.png --quit`. Floors also take `--sample=sea|villa|apse|cave`, `--restore=0.3`, `--fill-at=SECONDS`, `--finish-auto-lay`, `--zoom=`, `--center=x,y`.
- Pointing for screenshots: `--hover=X,Y` and `--click=X,Y` in interface pixels, at `--hover-at` and `--click-at` seconds. `--panel=new|settings|delete|dog|upgrades|chapters|achievements|letter` opens a panel on the title or workshop (`delete` asks to delete the first workshop, which needs "delete" typed; `letter` shows chapter 2's letter; `settings` opens at `--settings-tab=Sound|Display|Gameplay|Keys`), `--achievement[=ID]` slides in an achievement's card (the first one's without an ID), `--demo-state` opens a workshop in chapter 3 and `--max-state` one with every chapter, upgrade and stone, both never saved nor sent to Steam, `--ui-size=N` tries an interface size (-1 to 2) without keeping it, `--no-save` writes nothing, settings included, `--pick=N` picks a motif on the design table, `--add=MOTIF` drops one in the middle of it, `--guide` shows the first day's notes without a new workshop, and `--library-dir=PATH` points the library at a test folder (with `--no-save` the library is off). `--drag=X0,Y0,X1,Y1` presses and drags (at `--drag-at`). `--hour=H` sets the workshop's clock, `--resize-at=S` resizes the window once, `--dig-done` skips a restoration's digging, and `--gaps-left=N` then mends all but N gaps. `--kind=panel` gives the design table or reveal a picture panel, and with `--panels` the design table sets a panel into the floor (`--tab=pictures` opens that page) and the floor screen lays the sample's first motif as one. `--panel=bench` opens the workbench, `--day=N` sets the job board's day (5 is a festival), and `--visit` on the town map opens a visit to a floor (with `--room=ID`). `--kind=commission|copy|contest|restoration|free` gives the design table, client visit or reveal a test job of that kind (`free` opens free design); with `--room=ID` the reveal's test floor is moved into that room.
- Recording: add `--write-movie OUT.avi --fixed-fps 60 --quit-after FRAMES` before `--`.
- Trailer: `python tools/trailer/make_trailer.py` records the shots scripted in `game/tools/trailer/shots.gd` (the game runs one with `--trailer-shot=NAME`) and renders the cut in `game/tools/trailer/edit.gd` to `marketing/recordings/videos/steam_trailer/`, with an `.mp4` copy; `shots NAME` re-records one and `render` re-cuts; add `--cut=NAME` for one of the seven vertical shorts listed in `make_trailer.py`, whose shots are recorded in a tall frame. `tools/trailer/make_logo.py` draws the logo. `python tools/trailer/make_banner.py` writes `marketing/youtube_banner.png`, a 2560 by 1440 mosaic floor the game lays (`--icon-design=PATH.json --icon-res=WxH`) with the logo in the strip every screen shows.
- Steam store page: `python tools/steam/make_store_art.py` writes everything Steamworks asks for to `store/steam_page/`, the only folder it writes to (`README.txt` there says which file goes in which slot, beside the page's text files). The capsules and library hero are floors the game lays at the picture's exact size (`--icon-design`), with the logo laid over them at twice its pixel size (the small capsule uses `make_logo.compact_name()`, the name in smaller letters); the page background is the workshop rendered at one screen pixel to the game pixel with `--screen=workshop --no-hud --no-mouse --furnished --window=480x270` and enlarged three times; the screenshots are 1920 by 1080 frames of the recorded trailer shots. Everything it crops or combines goes in a temporary folder that is removed when it finishes. On the workshop `--no-hud` hides the top bar and letters and `--furnished` adds the plants, wine jars and lamps; `--no-mouse` ignores the real mouse, so nothing under it lights up.
- Build: `python tools/export.py` exports `builds/windows/Mosaicus Maximus.exe`, writes `Credits and licences.txt` beside it with `game/tools/write_credits.gd` (the credits from `data/credits.json`, then Godot's licence and its libraries'), and the launcher `Mosaicus Maximus - everything unlocked.bat`, which runs `--max-state`. The Windows preset uses a trimmed engine, `tools/engine/slim.windows.template_release.x86_64.exe` (about 32 MB instead of 107 MB), copied from Farmus Maximus, which builds it with its own `tools/engine/build_template.py`; `tools/engine/slim_profile.py` records what it leaves out. It has no 3D, physics, regex or noise module, so the game makes its own noise (`SoftNoise`). Check a new build by running `--run-tests` and `--flow=FOLDER` in the exported exe.

## Conventions

- Markdown docs: no em dashes, dollar signs written as `\$`, prices in CAD.
- Comments only for a non-obvious reason, one line, present tense.
- Clients are "he" or "she", following their portrait (`JobFactory.pronouns`), never "they".
- Commit only when Evan asks; he is the only author.
